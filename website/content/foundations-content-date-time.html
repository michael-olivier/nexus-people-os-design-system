Date and Time

Dates and times should be unambiguous in dashboards, notifications, and agent replies. Prefer formats that scan well in UI and defer locale-specific rendering to your i18n layer—not hand-typed strings.

Localization

Don’t hardcode formatted dates in components. Engineers should use locale-aware APIs so display matches the signed-in user’s settings. Designers specify which format length fits the layout; implementation handles how it renders.

Date formats

Nexus People OS specs default to US-style long dates in English mockups. Pick the shortest length that still reads clearly.

  • Full — Monday, June 12, 2026
  • Long — June 12, 2026
  • Medium — Jun 12, 2026 (tight spaces only)
  • Short / machine — 2026-06-12 (ISO 8601) for exports, filters, and logs—not primary display

Don’t use ordinal dates (June 12th). Avoid ambiguous numeric dates like 06/12/2026 in user-facing UI.

Date ranges

Use “to” between dates and years. Use “and” after “between”.

Do

2024 to 2025 · Between March and May

Don’t

2024–2025 (avoid for screen-reader clarity in UI copy)

Time formats

  • Use a colon between hours and minutes: 2:40 p.m.
  • Lowercase “a.m.” and “p.m.” with a space before them.
  • Prefer noon, midnight, or midday instead of 12:00 p.m. / a.m. when space allows.
  • 24-hour time is fine for ops, security, or system-driven views when locale settings require it.

Time ranges

Repeat a.m./p.m. only when the range crosses noon or midnight.

Example

10 a.m. to 2 p.m. · 6:30 to 10 p.m.

Relative time

Use friendly relative labels when precision matters less than recency—for example, activity feeds.

  • Just now · 5 minutes ago · Yesterday · 3 days ago
  • After about a week, show the absolute date (Jun 8, 2026).
  • Offer the full timestamp on hover or in a detail view when you show a relative label.

Say “every 2 weeks”, not “fortnightly”. Say “twice a year”, not “biannual”, unless the term is legally required.

Capitalization

Capitalize names of months and days. Capitalize named holidays and observances.
